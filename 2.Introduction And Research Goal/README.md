# Introduction and Research Goal

The growing use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot has created new opportunities for students to receive career guidance. These tools can help students explore career options, understand job requirements, identify necessary skills, and plan their future careers. However, many undergraduate students still experience difficulties in making career decisions due to a lack of information, uncertainty, and low confidence.

This study focuses on how Generative AI use for career guidance is associated with career decision-making difficulty among undergraduate students in Bangladesh. It also investigates two important mediating factors: **Career Exploration** and **Career Decision Self-Efficacy (CDSE)**. Career exploration helps students discover different career opportunities, while career decision self-efficacy reflects their confidence in making career-related decisions. Although previous studies have explored AI-based career guidance and traditional career support, limited research has examined these relationships among undergraduate students in Bangladesh.

The main goal of this research is to examine the relationship between Generative AI use for career guidance and career decision-making difficulty among undergraduate students in Bangladesh. Specifically, it aims to explore whether AI use is associated with greater career exploration and higher career decision self-efficacy, and whether these two factors mediate its relationship with career decision-making difficulty.

To achieve this goal, the study will conduct a quantitative survey of 384 undergraduate students from different universities and academic backgrounds in Bangladesh. The findings are expected to provide useful insights into the role of Generative AI in students' career exploration, decision-making confidence, and career planning.
