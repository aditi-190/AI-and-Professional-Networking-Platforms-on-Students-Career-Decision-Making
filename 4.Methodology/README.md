4.1 Research Design
This study adopted a quantitative, cross-sectional survey design to examine the associations among Generative AI use for career guidance, career exploration, career decision self-efficacy (CDSE), and career decision-making difficulty among undergraduate students in Bangladesh. A structured online questionnaire was used to collect primary data at a single point in time. The study follows a positivist, hypothesis-testing approach, and the individual student is the unit of analysis. Because of the cross-sectional design, the findings are interpreted as associations rather than causal effects.

4.2 Population and Sampling
The target population comprised undergraduate students enrolled in public and private universities in Bangladesh across different majors. Respondents were eligible if they were currently enrolled in an undergraduate program and were aged 18 years or above. Postgraduate students and incomplete responses were excluded.
Because a complete sampling frame of undergraduate students in Bangladesh is not available, a non-probability quota sampling technique was used. Quotas were set by university type (public/private), major, and gender to improve the diversity of the sample. Participants were recruited through [university groups, social media, and class networks]. The limitations of non-probability sampling for generalizability are acknowledged in Section 7.

4.3 Sample Size Determination
The minimum sample size was calculated using Cochran’s (1977) formula for large populations:
n = Z²p(1 − p) / e²
where Z = 1.96 (95% confidence level), p = 0.5 (maximum variability), and e = 0.05 (margin of error). This yields n = 384. This size also exceeds the commonly recommended minimum of 200 for structural equation modeling. 4.4 Measures
All constructs were measured with multi-item scales on a five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree), unless otherwise stated. Table 2 summarizes the measures.

Generative AI use for career guidance. No single validated scale exists for this construct, so items were adapted from prior studies on students’ use of AI chatbots for career-related tasks (e.g., Ahmed et al., 2025) and developed to capture three aspects: frequency of use, range of career-related purposes (e.g., exploring careers, choosing a major, preparing resumes and interviews), and reliance on AI advice. Its reliability and factor structure were examined in the pilot test and the main sample.
Career exploration. Career exploration was measured with [the selected subscales of] the Career Exploration Survey (Stumpf et al., 1983), which captures the extent to which students gather information about themselves and about occupations.
Career decision self-efficacy. CDSE was measured with the short form of the CDSE scale (Betz et al., 1996), which assesses confidence in completing tasks involved in career decision-making.
Career decision-making difficulty. Difficulty was measured with the [full/short] Career Decision-Making Difficulties Questionnaire (Gati et al., 1996), which covers lack of readiness, lack of information, and inconsistent information. Higher scores indicate greater difficulty. [Reverse-coded items were recoded before analysis.]

To reduce common method bias, participants were assured of anonymity and confidentiality, separate instructions were given for each section, and Harman’s single-factor test was conducted during analysis.
